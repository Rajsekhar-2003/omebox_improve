What Is OmeBox File Sharing?

OmeBox is a free, browser-based file sharing tool. You upload a file and get a shareable link, a QR code, and a 6-digit access code. There's no account, no email, and no app. You can send documents, photos, videos, ZIP archives, PDFs, or code from any device to any device, directly through your browser.

Think of OmeBox as a WeTransfer alternative without a signup step. Every share is temporary: you choose an expiry from 15 minutes to 3 days, and the files are deleted after that. That suits one-off transfers you don't want to leave online permanently, such as a client draft, a one-off invoice, or a photo you're moving between devices.

Whether you're sending a ZIP to a teammate, moving a photo from your phone to your laptop, or passing a signed PDF back to a client, OmeBox does it without registration.

How Secure Is OmeBox File Sharing?

Here is what OmeBox does and doesn't do, so you can decide what's safe to share:

  • Encrypted in transit. Uploads and downloads use HTTPS, so files are encrypted between your browser and OmeBox's servers.
  • No account or email. You don't give a name, email, phone number, or payment details to share a file.
  • Automatic expiry. Files are deleted from the servers when the expiry time you chose is reached.
  • Optional password. Turn on the password switch and the recipient must enter your password before downloading.
  • Cookies only with consent. Analytics and advertising scripts load only after you accept them in the cookie banner. See the Privacy Policy for what is collected.

One caveat: files are not end-to-end encrypted. For truly sensitive material such as financial records, IDs, legal documents under NDA, or password databases, use a dedicated end-to-end encrypted tool.

Tips for Safe File Sharing

No web tool can replace basic security habits. Follow these practices when sharing anything private:

  1. Use the shortest expiry that works. If the recipient is online now, choose 15 minutes. There's no reason to leave a private file available for 3 days.
  2. Use a password for anything private. A password keeps a leaked link from being useful. Send the password through a different channel than the link.
  3. Split the link and the code. Send the link by email and the 6-digit code by SMS, so one leaked message isn't enough.
  4. Check what's in your ZIP. Zipping a folder doesn't remove files you forgot were inside. Open the archive and check before uploading.
  5. Strip hidden data from photos. Photos can contain GPS location in their metadata. Remove it first if the location is private.
  6. Be careful with files from strangers. If you receive an .exe, .dmg, or .apk from someone you don't know, scan it (for example on VirusTotal) before opening it.

File Size Limits & Expiry Options Explained

OmeBox is designed for quick, temporary sharing, not as a replacement for cloud storage. Here's what to expect:

  • 100 MB per file. Enough for short video clips, high-resolution photos, full PDFs, and most ZIP archives. Larger files are rejected.
  • Up to 20 files per share. Upload a batch, such as an invoice set or a photo album, in one share with one code.
  • Expiry from 15 minutes to 3 days. Short for one-off transfers, longer for files a recipient may not open right away.
  • Automatic deletion. When the timer runs out, the files are deleted. If the recipient missed the window, you simply share again.

Need to send something larger than 100 MB? Use a cloud drive such as Google Drive, OneDrive, or Dropbox, which are built for large and permanent files.
